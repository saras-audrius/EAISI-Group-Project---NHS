Precision first — and who pays for each error

False positive

We tell a patient who would have done well that surgery may not help. They may forgo it. Unrecoverable. This is why precision wins.

False negative

We miss someone who will not improve much. They have the surgery, gain less than 7 points, and are counselled no differently than today. Bad, and survivable.

Then go live in 31_data_exploration / 42_threshold_analysis: missingness pattern, class balance, PR curve, and the SMOTE-vs-class-weights comparison that ends with "the balanced-weight variants were not better".
"The asymmetry chooses the metric. Nothing else does."

What an auditor actually asks for

The question, two years later

"Reconstruct the exact input this model was trained on, and show me who approved it."

Most teams cannot answer. Not because they were careless — because the answer was never written down anywhere it could be queried.

The MLflow tag scheme that answers it

gold_table + gold_table_version — the Delta version. "Trained on gold" becomes "trained on gold as of version 7", which time travel reconstructs exactly. This is the one they ask for.
feature_set_version — bump when the feature list changes, or two incomparable runs look comparable.
model_role — champion vs the challenger you keep for on-stage contrast.
approved_by — empty at training time, set later. Tags are mutable; that is precisely why approval is a tag and not a param.

"Params for what you compare, metrics for what you plot, tags for what you filter and later change. Get that split wrong and your experiment UI is unusable in a month — and your audit trail is unusable in a year."
Why FabricMLflow is the workspace's own experiment store. The lineage from Delta version to registered model version to scored row never leaves the estate — and never depends on someone's naming discipline in a folder of pickles.
